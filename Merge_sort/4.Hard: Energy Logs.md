Problem 4: Energy Logs

Difficulty: Hard

Problem Description

A robot operates for \(N\) consecutive minutes. During minute \(i\), its net energy change is \(E_i\):

If \(E_i>0\), the robot gains energy.
If \(E_i<0\), the robot consumes energy.
If \(E_i=0\), its energy does not change.

Researchers want to identify stable operating periods in the robot’s energy log.

A period must consist of one or more consecutive minutes. It is considered stable if its total net energy change is between \(L\) and \(U\), inclusive.

In other words, a period starting at minute \(a\) and ending at minute \(b\) is stable if:

$$ 1 \le a \le b \le N $$

and:

$$ L \le E_a+E_{a+1}+\cdots+E_b \le U $$

Determine the number of stable operating periods.

Two periods are considered different if they have different starting minutes or different ending minutes.

Input Specification

The first line contains three integers \(N\), \(L\), and \(U\).

The second line contains \(N\) space-separated integers \(E_1,E_2,\ldots,E_N\).

The following constraints apply:

$$ 1 \le N \le 200\,000 $$ $$ -10^9 \le E_i \le 10^9 $$ $$ -10^{14} \le L \le U \le 10^{14} $$
Output Specification

Output one integer: the total number of stable operating periods.

Sample Input 1
3 -2 2
-2 5 -1
Sample Output 1
3
Explanation of Sample Output 1

The following periods have total net energy changes within \([-2,2]\):

Starting Minute	Ending Minute	Total Net Energy Change
1	1	\(-2\)
3	3	\(-1\)
1	3	\(-2+5-1=2\)

All other periods have totals outside the required range.

Sample Input 2
4 0 0
0 0 0 0
Sample Output 2
10
Explanation of Sample Output 2

Every non-empty period has a total net energy change of 0.

There are:

4 periods of length 1.
3 periods of length 2.
2 periods of length 3.
1 period of length 4.

Therefore, the answer is:

$$ 4+3+2+1=10 $$

25%	\(N \le 5\,000\)
15%	\(E_i \ge 0\) for every \(i\).
40%	No additional constraints.

Note: Negative energy changes are allowed in the full test data. Both energy totals and the final answer may exceed the range of a signed 32-bit integer.
